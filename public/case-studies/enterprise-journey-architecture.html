From fragmented customer journeys to a funded onboarding transformation

01 Context

Journeys that grew organically, with no single enterprise view

A regulated commercial lending business needed to move from fragmented, inconsistently designed customer journeys to a coherent future state it could invest against. Journeys had grown organically across customer types and product lines, with no single enterprise view of how they worked, where they broke, or how they mapped to the organisation's capabilities.

The business needed a repeatable, data-led way to see the whole picture and prioritise where to act, grounded in its operating model, rather than another set of disconnected redesigns.

02 The challenge
Build one enterprise view of every customer journey and capability, decide objectively what to fix first, and carry that strategy through to a funded, compliance-critical delivery, without the work stalling between strategy and action.
03 Approach

A connected arc, from operating model to funded delivery

I began at the operating-model level, working directly with the CEO and executive team to assess the current state using a structured framework (McKinsey 7S) across structure, systems and the softer staff, skill and style dimensions. This identified gaps and duplication in both the organisation and the systems landscape, with recommendations taken to the board.

I then designed the approach for the customer journeys: a matrix across customer types, product categories and variants to structure the work, and a data-led method for prioritising which journeys to tackle first, using volume, value and customer drop-off. I established an enterprise-wide inventory of journey processes, facilitated organisation-wide workshops to surface pinch points, and built a business capability model identifying every function and service the business offered or intended to offer, with a current and target maturity assessment against each.

This surfaced commercial customer due diligence and onboarding as the priority for transformation. Once the platform (Newgen) was selected, I was the sole business architect on the delivery, translating the target journeys and capability model into the requirements the platform had to meet, defining the target onboarding process, and acting as the single bridge between the business and the implementation.
