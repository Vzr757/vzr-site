01 Context

Customer documentation, handled inconsistently across multiple systems

A UK financial services organisation was managing customer documentation on an ageing, unsupported platform. Documentation was handled inconsistently across multiple systems, with fragmented storage, indexing and transfer. Customer-facing documents were largely produced manually from templates, adding error risk and rework.

Unclear ownership of documents and access compounded the problem and drove duplicated effort. The estate was used daily by 300+ colleagues, making it a business-wide operational dependency rather than a contained system.

02 The challenge
Beyond replacing a legacy, unsupported document store, the organisation needed a defensible answer to a bigger question: what a modern document capability should look like, and how to get there without disrupting the process the organisation relied on every day.
03 Approach

Understand the need before the technology decision

We led the work to understand what the business actually needed before any technology decision was taken, identifying the core building blocks of the capability, what mattered most operationally, and using that to build a roadmap and select the right platform. We then led the migration onto the new, supported platform and the transition of daily users across to it.

Rather than replicate old ways of working, the new capability was embedded into the tools people already use, surfacing customer documents, such as statements, letters, contracts and correspondence, directly within Dynamics 365, Teams and Outlook, so documentation sits in the flow of work instead of a separate system.

As an extension once the platform was live, we ran a discovery to define how the new system should work going forward, covering where automation could remove manual effort and where AI could add value across document creation and classification.

04 Outcome

Off legacy, documents in the flow of work, and a roadmap ahead
